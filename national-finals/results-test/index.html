Event Schedule and Eligibility

  • The National Finals will be held September 11–12, 2026.
  • The event is played over two days using the Graham Marsh Golf Course and Arnold Palmer Golf Course.
  • The Tournament Committee will announce the final course order.
  • Participation is for qualified qualifier winners and eligible invitees approved by the Tournament Committee.
  • A slot is confirmed only after registration and payment, subject to capacity.

Eligibility: Players must be at least 16 years of age and must meet at least one of the following: (1) be a Filipino citizen, whether natural-born or naturalized; (2) be a permanent resident of the Philippines with a valid ACR card; or (3) be of Filipino descent.

3

Format and Scoring

The competition format is Net Stroke Play.

  • Net Score = Gross Score minus Course Handicap. Example: Gross 95, Course HCP 20 = Net 75.
  • Overall Score = Day 1 Net + Day 2 Net. Example: 75 + 71 = 146.
  • The lowest total Net Score wins, subject to the tie rules in these conditions.
4

Rules of Golf

  • Play is governed by the current Rules of Golf approved by The R&A and USGA.
  • The Official WAGC Referee governs play.
  • Competition disputes go first to the Official Referee and, if the referee is unavailable, second to the Tournament Director.

Double Par Pickup and Holing Out

Double Par Pickup applies only to Divisions D and E. Players in Divisions A, B and C must complete and hole out every hole and do not use Double Par Pickup.

For Divisions D and E, the following maximum score applies to each hole. Once the player reaches the applicable Double Par maximum, they must pick up and record the maximum score for that hole.

Par 36
Par 48
Par 510

Players Must Hole Out

Players in Divisions A, B, and C must hole out on every hole. Failure to properly hole out may result in a penalty or disqualification, as determined by the Tournament Committee or Head Referee.

Players in Divisions D and E must also hole out unless they have reached the applicable Double Par maximum for the hole. If a Division D or E player fails to hole out before completing the hole, the player may be assigned the applicable maximum score for that hole.

The Double Par maximums for Divisions D and E are:

  • Par 3 — 6 strokes
  • Par 4 — 8 strokes
  • Par 5 — 10 strokes

Pace of Play

Pace StandardExpected maximum time for 18 holes: 4 hours 54 minutes. Players are encouraged to make each stroke within 40 seconds.
  • If a flight is out of pace and a player is observed violating the rule, the entire flight receives a warning.
  • A later violation by any player in that same flight on a different hole results in a two-stroke penalty to the entire flight.

Bunkers Under Repair

When a bunker or a portion of a bunker is marked Ground Under Repair (GUR), the player receives free relief:

  • Outside the bunker, at the nearest point of complete relief;
  • No nearer the hole;
  • By dropping within one club-length.

The player does not have to take relief inside the bunker first.

Umbrella and Personal Assistants

Umbrella Assistants / Personal Assistants — Umbrella assistants are not allowed during tournament play. Personal assistants (except a player's caddy) may not give advice, touch or hold a player's equipment, stand on the putting surface, or assist with alignment. Any breach may result in penalties or disqualification as determined by the Committee. Personal assistants may help search for a ball.

Lost Ball / Out of Bounds — Model Local Rule E-5

Model Local Rule E-5 is in effect unless otherwise announced before play. For two penalty strokes, a player may take relief for a ball that is lost outside a penalty area or is out of bounds, using the following reference points and relief area:

Reference Points

  • Ball Reference Point: The point where the original ball is estimated to have come to rest on the course, or last crossed the edge of the course boundary to go out of bounds.
  • Fairway Reference Point: The point of fairway of the hole being played that is nearest to the Ball Reference Point, but is not nearer the hole than the Ball Reference Point.
  • For this Local Rule, "fairway" means any area of grass in the general area that is cut to fairway height or less. If the ball is estimated lost on the course or last crossed the course boundary short of the fairway, the Fairway Reference Point may be a grass path or a teeing ground for the hole being played cut to fairway height or less.

Relief Area

The relief area is between two reference lines: a line from the hole through the Ball Reference Point, and a line from the hole through the Fairway Reference Point. The relief area extends two club-lengths to the outside of each reference line.

  • The relief area must be in the general area and cannot be nearer the hole than the Ball Reference Point.
  • Once the player puts a ball in play under this Local Rule, the original ball is no longer in play and must not be played.
  • This remains true even if the original ball is found on the course before the end of the three-minute search period.

When E-5 Cannot Be Used

  • When the ball is known or virtually certain to have come to rest in a penalty area.
  • When the player has played another ball provisionally under the penalty of stroke and distance.

The player may use this Local Rule for a provisional ball that has not been found or is known or virtually certain to be out of bounds. The penalty for playing from a wrong place is governed by Rule 14.7a.

Preferred Lies

Throughout the National Finals, lift, clean and place is permitted in the general area:

  • Within one scorecard length;
  • No nearer the hole;
  • The action must be performed by the player; and
  • The caddie may not perform it.
13

Caddies on the Putting Green

Caddies are permitted to mark and lift a player's ball on the putting green when allowed under the Rules of Golf.

Caddies may provide putting advice and assist the player in reading the green. However, the player is responsible for aligning their own ball and establishing their own putting line.

A caddie may not align the player's ball for them or position the player for the stroke. The player must make their own final alignment before making the stroke.

14

Ties

Ties are decided by count-back in this order:

  1. Gross last 9 — holes 10–18
  2. Gross last 6 — holes 13–18
  3. Gross last 3 — holes 16–18
  4. Hole-by-hole beginning at Hole 18
  5. If still tied, compare the #1 handicap hole on the back nine, then #2, and so on until resolved.

For this 36-hole event, the scorecard playoff begins using the final round and works down according to the system above.

Dress Code and Personal Conduct

Dress Code

  • Collared shirts or mock turtlenecks are required.
  • Jeans, denim, jogging outfits and sweatpants are not allowed.

Personal Conduct

Players and guests must behave responsibly. Excessive anger, vulgar language, club throwing, alcohol abuse, illegal drug use, poor etiquette or disrespect may result in disqualification without refund.

16

Bad Weather

  • The Committee may suspend play for dangerous weather or an unplayable course.
  • Suspension and resumption signals will be announced by the Committee or through official event channels.
  • When play is suspended, players must mark their ball and stop immediately under Rule 5.7a. Failure to stop results in disqualification.
  • On resumption, play restarts from the exact spot.
  • If 18 holes cannot be completed, the Committee may assign scores to unplayed holes, use common completed holes, reschedule, or cancel.
  • If the common completed holes are eight holes or fewer, the entire round is cancelled.

Tee Time Format and Player Punctuality

Arrival TimePlayers should arrive one hour before their tee time and must be at the assigned teeing area at least 15 minutes before the scheduled tee time.

Sequential tee times will start from Hole 1 and Hole 10.

Late Arrival — Rule 5.3a

  • Within five minutes late: General Penalty / two strokes.
  • More than five minutes late: Double Par for every hole missed unless the Tournament Committee determines that exceptional circumstances apply.

Ready to Play

A player is ready to play when the player has at least one club, a ball, proper attire, and is prepared to make a stroke.

Handicap Cut-Off Date

The official 2026 National Finals handicap cut-off date will be September 1, 2026, or such later date as may be announced by the Tournament Committee.

The player's applicable Low Handicap Index as of the official cut-off date will be used for division placement and tournament handicap purposes. Course Handicap will then be calculated for the course being played.

Under WHS Rule 5.7, the Low Handicap Index is the lowest Handicap Index achieved over the 365-day period preceding the most recent score in the player's scoring record.

Caddie and Golf Cart Policy

  • One assigned caddie per player.
  • Caddies are assigned by the host venue and/or the Committee.
  • Outside caddies from another golf course are not allowed.
  • The Tournament Committee has the final say on assignment disputes.

Due to limited golf cart availability, solo carts are not allowed under any circumstances. All golf carts must be shared with a flight mate. Players who personally own a golf cart may use their own cart, subject to host venue approval and tournament operations, but must share it with a flight mate. Golf carts will otherwise be assigned or shared as directed by the host venue and Tournament Committee.
